# Color

## Where Sistema stands

Color in a Sistema project is built in three layers, and each is checked:

1. **Ramps.** Every color the system uses comes from a ramp of 19 stops generated by the palette tool. Nothing is picked by eye or computed by hand (`color.ramps.from-tool`).
2. **Roles.** Components never use a ramp stop directly. They use roles (`surface`, `on-surface`, `primary`, `surface-selected`), and each role points to a stop. Light and dark are two assignments of the same roles.
3. **Pairs.** Every place one color sits on another is a pair, and every pair that carries meaning is checked with the contrast tool, in every theme.

How the product looks is the person's choice, made at the direction: how much color, which accent, warm or cool grays. Sistema's position is about structure and checking, plus a few craft defaults (neutral ground with one accent, quiet states, status shown as detail) that the person can override.

```specimen
ramp #7B43E6 #72707A
Relay's accent (violet 550 from the palette library) and its grays, tinted toward the accent. Each strip runs from stop 50 to 950.
```

## Why ramps

Hand-picked values (`primary`, `primary-light`, `primary-dark`) work until the product needs a value nobody picked: a hover for a red button, a selected row, a dark theme. Then someone picks one more, outside the system, and the palette drifts. A full ramp has the value already.

Ramps from Sistema's palette tool add two properties that hand-picked sets don't have:

- **Stops mean the same thing in every hue.** The stops are placed by contrast against white, so stop 600 of blue and stop 600 of green have nearly the same contrast. A role that works on one hue works on another. This is the core idea of Stripe's accessible color work: choose colors by contrast, so hues are interchangeable ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems)).
- **Dark themes mirror cleanly.** The contrast curve is close to symmetric, so the stop at 1000 − *n* on a dark page has nearly the same contrast as stop *n* on a light one (see "Stops in a dark theme").

How the tool builds a ramp, and why, is in the palette generation guide. A contained marketing page or a short-lived prototype may not need full ramps; anything that will grow or carry a dark theme does.

### How ramps feed the semantic layer

Each role points to a stop, never to a free hex value. Typical starting points in a light theme, on Sistema's ramps:

| Role | Ramp and stop |
|---|---|
| `primary` | accent, about 550 to 600 (white text passes on it) |
| `primary-container` | accent, about 150, for chips and badges |
| `on-primary-container` | accent, about 750 |
| `surface` (the page) | neutral 50 |
| `surface-raised` (panels) | white, or neutral 50 on a white page |
| `on-surface` | neutral 850 |
| `on-surface-muted` | neutral 550 to 600 |
| `border` | neutral 200 to 250 |
| `border-strong` (control edges) | neutral 450 to 500 |

These are starting points. The approved composition at the direction already used stops for most roles, and the color play takes those stops from the specimen tool's `token_roles`. The pairs to check decide the rest.

## What every color system requires

Whatever its model, a complete color system has:

- **A foreground for every background.** Each surface has its text role (`on-*`), defined and checked together. This is what makes contrast checkable at all: the pair exists in the tokens, not only in a component.
- **The roles Sistema requires** (`color.roles.required`): primary and its container, secondary, the surface ladder (page, raised, overlay), text in three or four tones, borders, focus, the feedback colors, the state surfaces (hover and selected), a hover for every filled button, and the emphasis surfaces below.
- **Light and dark designed as one decision** (`opinion.both-themes`), not dark as an afterthought.
- **No raw values in components** (`tokens.usage.no-raw-values`). A hex value in a component is a color the system can't change, theme, or check.
- **Feedback colors reserved for meaning.** Red, amber, green, and blue carry status. Using them for decoration weakens the signal.

### Emphasis surfaces

Besides the surface ladder, every system Sistema builds has four emphasis surfaces, so a screen can let one thing lead instead of stepping evenly through levels (see the visual-language guide on emphasis):

| Role | Light theme, typically | Dark theme, typically |
|---|---|---|
| `surface-subtle` | A quiet zone or inset well inside a panel: the page tone, or one stop past it | The same: the page tone used inside a panel |
| `surface-featured` | The panel color; it leads by lift (the depth guide's featured shadow) | One step lighter than panels, plus the featured shadow |
| `surface-inverse` with `on-surface-inverse` | A near-black from the neutral ramp, with light text | A near-white, with dark text |
| `surface-brand` with `on-surface-brand` | A deep stop of the accent ramp, deep enough for light text | The same, or a step lighter, still with light text |

- **Quiet levels take small steps.** Neighboring stops near white (and near black in dark themes) are close on purpose; pick quiet surfaces from them, not from the middle of the ramp.
- **Emphasis takes big steps.** Inverse and brand surfaces sit far from the page, so they read as blocks, not as another level.
- **States must be visible where they're used.** Hover, selected, and highlighted fills sit at least one full stop from every surface they appear on (white counts as the step before 50), and differ from each other (`color.states.visible`). Hover at 50 on white panels is too faint; use 100.
- **Containers are for small marks.** Feedback and accent containers (`*-container`) fill chips and badges. Messages and banners sit on a neutral surface with the status color in the icon and title (`opinion.status-as-detail`).
- **Shadows on color read differently.** Fluent adjusts shadow strength by the surface's lightness for this reason ([Fluent](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/elevation)).

Polaris names surfaces by prominence and has brand and inverse families in both large-area and small-area versions ([Polaris tokens](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@shopify/polaris-tokens@9.4.2/dist/esm/src/themes/base/color.mjs)); Material has inverse surfaces and containers for the same jobs.

## How much color

How far the accent reaches is the first taste question at the direction (the visual-language guide shows the options). Each answer sets how colorful the surfaces and grays may be. The numbers are OKLCH chroma, on the scale the palette tool reports (0 is gray; the most saturated screen colors reach about 0.37). They are Sistema's starting guidance, not limits, and the rule `quality.color.commitment-level` checks the tokens against the answer the person chose.

| Answer | Where the accent appears | Surfaces and grays | Accent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet | The main button, checkboxes, focus, links | Up to about 0.015 | About 0.08 to 0.16 |
| Brand | Also the navigation's current item | About 0.01 to 0.03, with the grays leaning toward the accent | About 0.12 to 0.20 |
| Tinted | A deep accent block carries the navigation; the page takes a faint tint | About 0.03 to 0.06 | About 0.15 to 0.25, often with a second hue |

A fourth option, color as the dominant element (saturated backgrounds), suits some marketing and brand pages. Sistema uses it only when the person asks, and it needs every pair checked by hand: the usual dark mirroring stops working at that strength.

Selection is a neutral step under every answer, and whether a screen has a brand block is decided per screen in DESIGN.md's "What stands out" list.

### Choosing the accent

- **A brand color the project already has** is a fixed point. Use it.
- **Otherwise, propose from the palette library** (`{{sistema_url}}/palettes/library.json`): a colorful palette at one of its bright middle stops, 400 to 600, where the hue is at its most saturated. Show two or three as options.
- **Check it against the category default.** Blue for finance, green for health, violet for developer tools: the first-order reflex in the visual quality guide. A default can still be right, but say why.
- **Keep it apart from the status hues** when status matters. An accent near the info blue makes selected rows read as a status.

```specimen
ramp #DE3B35 #7B43E6 #255FE0
Three library palettes. In each strip the hue is strongest around stops 450 to 600, which is where an accent seed comes from.
```

## Neutral palette design

The grays set every surface, border, and gray text, so their temperature shows everywhere, even where no accent does.

- **Cool** grays (a little blue) read technical and crisp.
- **Warm** grays (a little yellow or red) read softer, more editorial.
- **Tinted toward the accent** makes the interface feel of a piece. Radix pairs each accent with a matching gray family (mauve, slate, sage, olive, sand) for the same reason ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/themes/docs/theme/color)).
- **Nearly neutral** (a trace of temperature) is the quietest. A gray with no temperature at all reads clinical, which is why the color bans ask for some (`quality.color.bans`).

The page should be an off-white or off-black, not pure white or black; white panels on an off-white page are fine. Add pure white or black to `palette.json` as `palette.base.white` and `palette.base.black` when a role uses them.

## The decision framework

Mature systems organize color in four ways. They can be combined, and most systems Sistema builds use the first two together: generated ramps underneath, named roles on top.

| Model | How it works | Used by | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tonal palettes | A few key colors generate tones from black to white in a perceptual space; roles take tones, and dark themes take other tones | Material (HCT) | Generating themes from one brand color, user-chosen colors |
| Step scales | Each color is a fixed number of steps with set jobs (backgrounds, borders, solid fills, text) | Radix (12 steps) | Libraries where consumers pick their own accent |
| Named palettes | A curated set of named hues, each a scale, mapped to roles | Ant Design, Carbon, Tailwind | Explicit brand control, several brands over one palette |
| Layered tokens | Surfaces are named by layer (`layer-01`, `layer-02`), and components read the layer they sit on | Carbon | Dense tools with many stacked panels |

How to choose:

- **Need roles to work across any hue, and dark themes without hand-picking?** Generated ramps under roles. This is Sistema's default.
- **Many stacked surfaces** (nested panels, drawers, editors)? Add layer-relative surface roles on top.
- **Several brands over one system?** Keep the roles fixed and swap the ramps each brand points to.
- **A consumer-chosen accent** (a library others theme)? Step-scale jobs make "any color in this slot" predictable.

## Dark mode: the tonal shift

Dark mode is not an inversion. Inverting lightness gives harsh contrast, surfaces that can't step, and saturated colors that glow on black. Material's tonal approach pairs light-theme tones with mirrored dark tones (a primary at tone 40 in light becomes tone 80 in dark) ([Material](https://m3.material.io/styles/color/system/how-the-system-works)). Sistema follows the same idea on its own ramps, with one difference for surfaces.

The decision comes first and stays the same in both themes: if a selected row is a gray step with an accent edge in light, it is in dark too (`opinion.both-themes`). Only the stops change.

### Stops in a dark theme

On ramps from Sistema's palette tool, text and fills mirror cleanly, but surfaces don't.

- **Text and fills: mirror the stop.** A role at stop *n* on the light page has nearly the same contrast at stop 1000 − *n* on the dark page. On the neutral ramp, stop 550 on stop 50 and stop 450 on stop 950 differ by about 0.1 in contrast ratio. Mirroring is the starting point; the contrast tool still checks every pair.
- **Surfaces: match the step, not the stop.** The light theme's quiet step is page 50 to white panels, smaller than any step between two stops. Mirrored stop numbers (page 950, panels 850) make the dark step about seven times larger, and quiet panels then compete with the featured item. Step one stop at a time instead (`color.dark.surface-steps`):

| Role | Light theme | Dark theme |
|---|---|---|
| Page (`surface`) | 50 | 950 |
| Panel (`surface-raised`) | white | 900 |
| Quiet zone or well (`surface-subtle`) | 50, the page tone | 950, the page tone |
| Featured (`surface-featured`) | white, on the featured shadow | 850, plus the featured shadow |
| Menus and dialogs (`surface-overlay`) | white, on the overlay shadow | 800, plus the overlay shadow |
| Hover (`surface-hover`) | 100: one full stop past white panels | one stop lighter than the panel, and distinct from the menu (750 on menus) |
| Selected (`surface-selected`) | 150: one stop past hover | one stop past hover |

The dark steps are still larger than the light ones, because the ramp has no finer stop at that end, and a little extra helps where shadows barely show. The order of strength is the same in both themes: panels lift least, then the featured item, then floating layers.

```specimen
surfaces accent=#7B43E6 neutral=tinted separation=shadow
Relay's surfaces in both themes: the page, a panel with a hovered and a selected row, the featured item, and a menu with a highlighted item. Each label names the stop.
```

## States and containers

- **Hover** is a small tone change, **selected** a neutral step past hover with the accent only as a detail (a checked box, an edge), and **focus** a ring in the focus color (`opinion.quiet-states`). A selection filled with the accent turns into a saturated band in a dark theme and outshouts status.
- **Every filled button role has a hover** (`primary-hover`, plus secondary and error when used), from the same ramp, not from opacity.
- **Containers** (`primary-container`, `error-container`) are for small marks. Messages put their status in the icon and title on a neutral surface.

## Pairs to check

Most contrast failures in Sistema's reference runs were not in the role table but in states and components: text on a selected row, a checkbox edge on the selected fill, a destructive item on a menu highlight, the focus ring on a highlight. Check these pairs in every theme, with the contrast tool's `purpose` set to what each pair is for:

| Where | Foreground | Background | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every surface (page, panel, subtle, featured, menu, inverse, brand) | each text tone used on it, links, feedback text | the surface | text |
| Same surfaces | control edges, the focus ring, icons that carry meaning | the surface | ui |
| Hover, selected, and highlight fills | the text, muted text, links, and control edges that sit on them | the state fill | text or ui |
| Menus | each item's text on the highlight, including destructive items | the highlight | text |
| Filled buttons, and their hover | the button's text and icon | the fill | text |
| Chips and badges | the word, and the dot beside it | the container | text; the dot informational when the word carries the status |
| Messages | the title and icon in the status color | the message surface | text |
| Checkboxes, switches, radios | the edge when off, the fill when on | every surface they sit on, including selected rows | ui |
| Dividers and decorative edges | the line | the surface | informational |

When a stop changes later, re-check every pair that uses it, including these.

WCAG 2.x measures contrast by luminance and overstates it for some pairs near black. APCA, a candidate method for a future WCAG, models perception more closely ([APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCAeasyIntro)). Sistema checks against the standard the project chose, and the squint check below catches pairs that pass but read poorly.

## Worked example: Relay

Relay is a dispatch board for a trades business. Status has to be the strongest signal, and dispatchers look at the screen all day.

**The direction.** The person chose Quiet color, an accent from the palette library (violet 550, `#7B43E6`) over the proposed purple, and grays tinted toward violet (seed `oklch(0.55 0.015 293)`, which the tool resolved to `#72707A`). Petrol, the first proposal, was rejected, which is how accent seeds came to be taken from the library.

**The roles**, from Relay's `tokens/src/color.json` and `color.dark.json`:

| Role | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| `surface` | neutral 50 | neutral 950 |
| `surface-raised` | white | neutral 900 |
| `surface-featured` | white | neutral 850 |
| `surface-overlay` | white | neutral 800 |
| `surface-hover` | neutral 100 | neutral 850 |
| `surface-selected` | neutral 150 | neutral 800 |
| `on-surface` | neutral 850 | neutral 50 |
| `on-surface-muted` | neutral 600 | neutral 350 |
| `primary` | accent 550 | accent 450 |
| `primary-container` | accent 150 | accent 800 |
| `border` | neutral 200 | neutral 750 |
| `border-focus` | accent 500 | accent 400 |

**What review changed.** The first build mirrored the light stops for dark surfaces (page 950, panels 850). Panels then stood out more in dark than in light, which led to stepping one stop at a time. The selected row was the accent at low strength, and the inline messages were filled with their status color: both fine in light, both heavy saturated blocks in dark. They became a gray step with an accent edge, and gray messages with colored icons and titles, in both themes. That review is where "one decision for both themes" came from.

**The evidence.** 150 role pairs and 128 component pairs, checked with the contrast tool in both themes, all passing; the lowest text pair was about 5.0:1 under WCAG 2.2 AA.

## Failure modes

- **The category's obvious accent,** chosen because it's familiar, not because it serves the product.
- **Dark surfaces by mirrored stop numbers,** so quiet panels step about seven times as far as in light and compete with the featured item.
- **Accent fills for selection,** which turn into saturated bands in a dark theme.
- **State colors invisible where they're used:** hover at 50 on white panels, a menu highlight equal to the menu's own color.
- **Opacity-derived colors** (`primary/80`), which change with whatever is underneath and can't be checked.
- **Checking only the role table,** and missing the pairs that states and components create.
- **Status colors used for decoration,** so they stop meaning anything.

## Checking the work

- **The contrast tool,** with every pair in "Pairs to check", in every theme, and `purpose` set per pair.
- **State visibility** (`color.states.visible`): hover, selected, and highlighted fills against every surface they appear on.
- **Chroma against how much color** the direction chose (`quality.color.commitment-level`).
- **Squint at a screen in each theme,** or blur it. Status and the primary action should read first, quiet panels last. If a panel or a selection reads before status, a step is too big.
- **The token check page** shows the colors in use (rows, chips, messages, a menu) in each theme, not only as swatches.

## Plays

- `color.scheme` builds the ramps and roles, and checks the pairs.
- `color.map-existing` brings existing colors into the system.
- `visual-language.direction` asks the color questions and records the answers.
