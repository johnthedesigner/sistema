Palette: current and proposed

Every ramp below is generated twice from the same seed: once by the current algorithm, once by the proposed one. Three fixed rules change; each is set by one named constant in src/lib/palette-next.ts, so the result stays deterministic and explainable. Hover any swatch for its values.

1. An eased contrast curve

Stops are still placed by contrast against white, from 1.01 to 19, and stop 500 is unchanged. The curve eases in and out, so steps are fine near white and black, where page and panel tones live, and wider through the mid-tones. EASE = 0.45

2. Colorfulness by lightness, the same for every hue

The seed sets how colorful a ramp is. One shared curve sets how colorfulness rises from white (in proportion to distance from white), holds through the mid-tones (L 0.32 to 0.8, or up to the seed's own lightness for bright seeds such as yellow), and eases toward black. Where a hue can't reach it on screen, chroma approaches the limit smoothly, so no single stop is adjusted on its own.

3. Yellows lean warm as they darken

Dark yellow doesn't exist on screens; held at the same hue it can only become olive. Hues within 38° of 92° turn toward orange in proportion to how dark the stop is, up to 34° at black, the way ochres and browns behave.

Rule 1: finer steps at both ends

Target contrast against white for each stop. Dashed is the current, evenly spaced curve; solid is the eased one. The current curve jumps from 1.04 to 1.19 between stops 50 and 100, which is why there was no quiet page tone.

Rule 2: tints with equal weight

Status chips use a light tint behind stop 800 text: stop 100 today; in the proposal, stop 150, because the eased curve makes 100 lighter and 150 now sits where 100 did (1.17:1 against 1.19). Today green's tint is about four times as colorful as red's or blue's, because each hue's chroma is scaled to that hue's own on-screen maximum. Proposed, every hue follows the same colorfulness curve.
