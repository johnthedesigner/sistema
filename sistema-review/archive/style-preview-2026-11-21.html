A look for Sistema's site

Five questions, each shown on Sistema's own guide page. Pick an option for each, or keep the proposals, then copy your answers from the bar at the bottom and paste them back to me. The logo colors stay, and blue stays the accent.

What the site needs
  • Long guides are comfortable to read, with clear headings and tables.
  • The one action that matters, copying a prompt, is never in doubt.
  • Reference (plays, rules, opinions) is easy to scan and link to.
  • The brand is recognizable without competing with the content.
  • The site shows the craft it teaches, in light and dark.

Your answers: brand color presence, grays tinted toward blue (proposed: warm), 8px corners with round chips, panels separated by tone, Inter with Fraunces headings.

It won't look like: a generic documentation template, a blue-on-white corporate site, or a page of outlined boxes.

Question 1 of 5

How much color?

How far the blue reaches beyond buttons and focus. Yellow and red stay for the logo and brand moments.

Why: Readers move between guides, plays, and reference; marking where they are helps, and the brand stays recognizable without competing with the text.

Question 2 of 5

Warm or cool grays?

The grays set every surface, border, and secondary text.

Why: Your answer (the proposal was warm). Ties the grays to the brand blue.

Question 3 of 5

How round?

The corner radius of buttons and fields. Everything else scales from it. Chips are fully round.

Why: Calm and current without feeling soft; it suits both reading pages and tools.

Question 4 of 5

How do panels separate?

How cards, callouts, and panels stand off the page.

Why: Reading pages stay calm with few lines; tables and code keep the lines that help.

Question 5 of 5

Which typefaces?

A sans for the interface and text, and a serif for headings. The serif carries the brand voice on an editorial site.

Why: Recognizably Sistema already; Inter reads well in dense reference, and Fraunces gives headings personality.

All together

Every proposal at once, on a guide page and the home page, in light and dark. Content is arranged by role; the real pages come in the site restructure.
